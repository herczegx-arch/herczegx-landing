Callboard

A production system, in live use

What I could not find ready-made, I built. It now runs the theatre's daily operation.

The problem

Production planning lived in spreadsheets and email. The rehearsal schedule went out as an attachment, and from that moment nobody knew who had opened it, who had seen the change, or which version was current. A call sheet that never arrives is not an administrative nuisance: it is a crew member who does not come to work.

The solution

I modelled the system in a spreadsheet first, because showing what I wanted was easier than explaining it. That became the web version, and today it is three applications on one shared backend.

applicationwhat it does
Callboardproductions, rehearsal schedule, call sheets, distribution, budget
Callworkweekly rosters and time records for the technical departments
CallMemomanagement tasks, decisions, annual plan
viewer pagea shareable rehearsal schedule with no login

Crew members install nothing. The personal link in their email opens on their phone, and if they add it to the home screen it behaves like an app from then on.

What a spreadsheet cannot do

Distribution tells the truth. Nothing is marked as sent until the email actually goes out. There is no state where the system says delivered and reality says otherwise.

Three questions, three answers. When it went out and for which day. Whether anything changed since. And exactly what changed, field by field.

People who never opened it are visible. By name. That is where the office picks up the phone. The machine does not judge: a human decides what counts as a substantive change.

Every distribution is archived. A PDF, with the sender and the time it went out.

Push notifications carry no content. Nothing from the rehearsal schedule passes through third-party servers.

Not built for one theatre

Venues, roles, the address book, the technical departments and the rehearsal types are settings, not hard-coded values. What is tied to one house today is the branding: the name and the logo sit in the code, so another company swaps those instead of rewriting the logic. That is why the screenshots carry no theatre name and no logo.

What building it taught me

The channel will always be unreliable. Email dies, notifications are not guaranteed, the phone stays silent. The answer is not a better channel, it is making the gap visible in time. The final channel is a person: the office calls.

Whoever reports a fault is right. I sent back four findings after the first live test. All four were real, and the worst was silent data loss, not a display glitch.

What goes out to everyone at once cannot be taken back. I approved the introductory letter to the company sentence by sentence. The tests now guard those approved sentences.

Status

Live, in daily use. The distribution and notification chain is finished; the rollout is in progress.
