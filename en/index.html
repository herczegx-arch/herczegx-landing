Behind the call board

Three venues, five premieres a year, a 228-seat house where one empty evening hurts. My day starts with a walk through the building: mornings are rehearsals and planning, afternoons meetings, evenings I stay until the performance starts.

Film taught me what theatre sometimes forgets: the crew is not support staff, it is one half of the performance. No director and no star, however great, stages a play alone.

What I measure by

Authenticity, integrity, concept. Kitsch is what is there for its own sake; taste is what has a concept behind it. A tight budget is not a burden, it forces the better idea, but vision alone is not enough without the how: execution, money, deadline, the person responsible.

How I lead

I hold a high standard and expect the same from others. I settle problems face to face with the person involved, never behind their back. As long as someone does their job well, they have no trouble with me.

Why theatre

Any content can be generated now with AI. Theatre is the last place where real emotion from real people reaches a real audience and, for one evening, turns it into a community. AI cannot render that.

[ EXPERTISE ]

Where the practice sits

Seven areas. Each one backed by work, not a certificate.

Season plan, rehearsal period, weekly and daily schedules, call sheets. I build the sequence backwards from opening night, with an owner and a deadline. The weekly schedule informs, the daily one obliges - mixing the two is how fifty people come to work on the wrong day.

Sound economics is not high revenue, it is keeping the budget you planned. Done well, something is left over, and that pays for a bonus, a repair, a refurbishment. With public money, clean accounting is not caution: there is real delivery behind every invoice.

I do not enjoy confrontation, but when the order of a rehearsal is at stake I will do it. I have sent someone out of the building, and I have sat down next to someone so my presence would keep them from losing it. Hierarchy entitles nobody to shout.

Daily operation across three sites: main stage, rehearsal room, external venue. I start the day with a walk through the building. What breaks technically can cost tonight’s performance, so it cannot wait until tomorrow.

The old ways of selling tickets are gone and the supply is enormous and still growing. A small art theatre only stays visible if it approaches its audience differently. Theatre is made for the audience, not the critics.

As a line producer I ran television series and commercials. Day-by-day shooting schedules, hard cost control, crew logistics. That habit brought day-level planning and real data tracking into my theatre work.

A spreadsheet model first, then a web system, and today three applications in live use. I did not train as a developer, but service design is close to me; I built the system because my own work needed it.
