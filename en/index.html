About

Behind the call board

I am Head of Production at the Radnóti Miklós Theatre in Budapest. In practice that means I own the full life of a production: from the first steps through opening night to the daily set changes and the maintenance that follows. Three venues, five premieres a year, more than two hundred performances, and a 228-seat house where one empty evening hurts.

My day starts with a walk through the building. Mornings are rehearsals and rehearsal planning, afternoons are meetings, and in the evening I stay until the performance starts. Sometimes I sit in and watch.

What I measure by

Authenticity, integrity, concept. Kitsch is what is there for its own sake; taste is what has a concept behind it. The same test applies to a gag, a set and a poster: anything unjustified is empty.

A tight budget is not a burden. It forces the better idea. An empty stage can be magnificent if there is a vision behind it. But vision alone is not enough without the how: the execution, the money, the deadline and the person responsible.

I do not believe in the sanctity of tech week. Work through the whole rehearsal period with a plan and with discipline, and you will not be scrambling in the last one.

How I lead

I hold a high standard and expect the same from others. I settle problems face to face with the person involved, never behind their back. When something grinds, I step in. I am patient, but not indefinitely.

People often think I am a hard man to work with. As long as someone does their job well, they have no trouble with me.

Why theatre

Any content can be generated now with AI. Video, voice, a face. Theatre is the last place where real emotion from real people reaches a real audience and, even if only for one evening, turns it into a community. AI cannot render that.

Expertise

Where the practice sits

Seven areas. Each one backed by work, not a certificate.

Production planning and scheduling

Season plan, rehearsal period, weekly and daily schedules, call sheets. I build the sequence backwards from opening night, with an owner and a deadline. The weekly schedule informs, the daily one obliges: mixing the two is how fifty people come to work on the wrong day.

Budget and cost control

Sound economics is not high revenue, it is keeping the budget you planned. Done well, something is left over, and that pays for a bonus, a repair, a refurbishment. With public money, clean accounting is not caution: there is real delivery behind every invoice.

Leading crews, handling conflict

I do not enjoy confrontation, but when the order of a rehearsal is at stake I will do it. I have sent someone out of the building, and I have sat down next to someone so that my presence would keep them from losing it. Hierarchy entitles nobody to shout.

Venue operations

Daily operation across three sites: main stage, rehearsal room, external venue. I start the day with a walk through the building. What breaks technically can cost tonight's performance, so it cannot wait until tomorrow.

Marketing and sales

The old ways of selling tickets are gone and the supply is enormous and still growing. A small art theatre only stays visible if it approaches its audience differently. Theatre is made for the audience, not for the critics.

Film production

As a line producer I ran television series and commercials. Day-by-day shooting schedules, hard cost control, crew logistics. That habit brought day-level planning and real data tracking into my theatre work.

Building systems

A spreadsheet model first, then a web system, and today three applications in live use. I did not train as a developer, but service design is close to me; I built the system because my own work needed it.
